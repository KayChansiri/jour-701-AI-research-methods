## Course Description

Research methods are changing as computation, large language models, and increasingly accessible analytical tools become part of everyday research.

In this course, you will learn how to move from a research question to evidence: reviewing literature, selecting appropriate research designs, organizing and analyzing data, interpreting findings, evaluating AI-assisted workflows, and communicating results.

The course combines traditional research methodology with modern research tools, including Python, Google Colab, pretrained language models, and generative AI. Programming is treated as a research tool rather than as an end in itself. No prior programming experience is required.

**Tools can assist research, but they cannot replace methodological judgment.**

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the course, you should be able to:

1. Explain the major stages of the research process and connect research questions to evidence, design, analysis, and interpretation.
2. Explain foundational concepts behind contemporary AI and large language models at a level relevant to applied research.
3. Use Google Colab and basic Python to conduct reproducible research tasks.
4. Critically evaluate the use of AI and pretrained language models in research workflows.
5. Develop research questions and hypotheses grounded in scholarly literature.
6. Distinguish among major quantitative research designs, including surveys, experiments, A/B tests, and quasi-experiments.
7. Identify appropriate sampling, measurement, and data-collection strategies.
8. Select and conduct introductory statistical analyses including correlation, t-tests, regression, ANOVA, and chi-square.
9. Apply introductory computational approaches to text data, including sentiment and text classification.
10. Interpret analytical results in substantive rather than purely statistical terms.
11. Identify ethical, validity, and reproducibility concerns associated with computational and AI-assisted research.
12. Communicate research findings to academic, industry, and other stakeholder audiences.

## Course Structure

### Block 1 — Modern Research Tools in the Age of AI

You will begin by examining how AI and computation are changing contemporary research. Topics include large language models, Transformer foundations, Google Colab, Python fundamentals, pretrained language models, and human evaluation of AI-assisted research workflows.

### Block 2 — Data Collection and Research Design

You will move from tools to research decisions, including research questions and hypotheses, sampling, measurement, surveys, randomized experiments, A/B testing, quasi-experimental designs, and validity.

### Block 3 — Data Analysis and Interpretation

You will learn how data are structured, prepared, analyzed, and interpreted. Topics include data cleaning, correlation, t-tests, linear regression, ANOVA, chi-square, and introductory computational text and sentiment classification.

Most analytical topics follow a **concept → guided application** structure, emphasizing methodological reasoning and interpretation over extensive programming.

### Block 4 — Responsible Research and Communication

You will integrate analytical reasoning with AI ethics, reproducibility, human oversight, limitations, and stakeholder communication.

The course concludes with an applied group research project in which you use an instructor-provided dataset, select an appropriate analytical approach, interpret the results, and communicate what the evidence means.

## Week-by-Week Course Schedule

| Week              | Topic                                          | Focus                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Week 1**        | Research Methods in the Age of AI              | Research workflows, contemporary research tools, large language models, and the role of computational methods in modern research                                                             |
| **Week 2**        | Inside Large Language Models                   | From NLP to Transformers, tokenization, embeddings, attention, inference, and limitations of language models                                                                                 |
| **Week 3**        | Python and Computational Research Environments | Google Colab, Python fundamentals, variables, data types, lists, functions, libraries, and reproducible computational workflows                                                              |
| **Week 4**        | Python Meets Pretrained Language Models        | Accessing pretrained models, basic model inference, human + AI literature screening, classification, and evaluating model judgments                                                          |
| **Week 5**        | Sampling and Measurement                       | Populations, samples, sampling strategies, variables, operationalization, reliability, validity, and measurement decisions                                                                   |
| **Week 6**        | Survey and Experimental Research               | Survey design, randomized controlled experiments, A/B testing, independent and dependent variables, manipulation, and random assignment                                                      |
| **Week 7**        | Quasi-Experimental Research                    | Quasi-experimental designs, confounding, internal and external validity, and translating research questions into defensible study designs                                                    |
| **Week 8**        | Data Structure and Cleaning                    | DataFrames, observations and variables, missing data, recoding, outliers, analytical assumptions, pandas, and NumPy                                                                          |
| **Week 9**        | Correlation and Relationships                  | Covariation, strength and direction of relationships, interpreting correlation, and connecting statistical evidence to research questions                                                    |
| **Week 10**       | t-Tests and Linear Regression                  | Comparing means, modeling relationships, regression coefficients, statistical controls, model interpretation, and Python implementation                                                      |
| **Week 11**       | Analysis of Variance                           | One-way ANOVA, between- and within-group variability, F statistics, effect sizes, post-hoc comparisons, and interpretation                                                                   |
| **Week 12**       | Categorical Data and Chi-Square                | Contingency tables, observed and expected frequencies, chi-square tests of independence, effect sizes, and categorical inference                                                             |
| **Week 13**       | Computational Text Analysis                    | Text as data, sentiment analysis, computational classification, pretrained language models, labels, model outputs, and validation                                                            |
| **Week 14**       | Sentiment and Text Classification Lab          | Applying computational text-classification workflows, comparing approaches, evaluating errors, and interpreting model-generated labels                                                       |
| **Week 15**       | Final Project Development                      | Working with instructor-provided datasets, selecting research questions, identifying variables, choosing analytical approaches, and planning the final workflow                              |
| **Week 16**       | Responsible AI-Assisted Research               | AI ethics, reproducibility, limitations, human oversight, responsible interpretation, and communicating findings to stakeholders                                                             |
| **Final Project** | Stakeholder Research Presentation              | Conducting an analysis in Python/Google Colab, interpreting results, demonstrating the analytical workflow, discussing AI-assisted coding, and presenting findings to a stakeholder audience |

The progression is intentionally cumulative: you first develop computational and AI literacy, then learn how to design research, then apply analytical methods, and finally integrate those skills into a reproducible and responsibly communicated research project.

## Technology

You will work with:

* **Google Colab** for browser-based computational notebooks
* **Python** for introductory data management and analysis
* **pandas and NumPy** for working with structured data
* **statistical Python libraries** for introductory quantitative analysis
* **pretrained language models** for guided computational text activities
* **generative AI** for selected research and coding activities
* **GitHub** for openly accessible instructional and computational materials

No prior programming experience is required.

You are not expected to memorize Python syntax or become an AI engineer. The emphasis is on understanding **what computational tools are doing, when they are appropriate, and how their outputs should be evaluated**.

## Course Readings

There is **no required textbook to purchase**.

Research methodology, computational tools, and AI are evolving quickly, and no single textbook adequately covers the full scope of the course. Readings are therefore selected from freely available books, scholarly articles, technical documentation, and instructor-provided materials.

One frequently used resource is:

**Ethan Weed, *Learning Statistics with Python***

Specific readings are assigned according to the topics covered each week. You are not expected to read an entire technical book from beginning to end.

## AI and Human Oversight

AI is intentionally incorporated into selected course activities.

You will learn to use AI systems as **research tools whose outputs require evaluation**, rather than as substitutes for research judgment.

Depending on the activity, AI may support:

* literature organization;
* classification;
* coding;
* debugging;
* computational analysis; or
* other research workflows.

You remain responsible for:

* verifying AI-generated information and code;
* understanding the analyses you conduct;
* identifying errors and limitations;
* protecting sensitive information;
* documenting AI use when appropriate; and
* making the final methodological and interpretive decisions.
